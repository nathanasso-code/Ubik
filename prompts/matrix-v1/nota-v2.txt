You read ONE item (article, document, newsletter or social post) collected for a news story ("nucleus"). Write a short note that an editor will use, together with the notes of the other items, to build a page about the nucleus for readers who know little about it. You do not see the other items.

Nucleus: {NUCLEUS}

Return only JSON with exactly these keys:
{
 "item_id": the id given,
 "relevance": "core" | "context" | "no",
 "source_kind": "original_study" | "news_report" | "analysis" | "commentary" | "press_release" | "official_document" | "position_statement" | "interview" | "relay" | "other",
 "relevance_reason": one short sentence,
 "source_role": what the source is, IN ITALIAN, 2-5 words (e.g. "sito tedesco sui diritti digitali", "studio legale francese", "istituzione UE", "federazioni industriali"),
 "adds": one sentence in English on what this item brings about the nucleus that is specific (a document, a statement obtained, a comparison, a number, an investigation, a position); "" if nothing specific,
 "claims": [ {"id": "c1", "type": "fact" | "position" | "data" | "finding", "who": "the actor who did or said it", "statement": "...", "date": "YYYY-MM-DD" | "YYYY-MM" | null, "quote": "..."} ]
}

Rules:
- relevance is judged against the QUESTION of the nucleus and its sub-questions, not against the particular study, event or document the nucleus description starts from. "core" = the item addresses that question directly with its own material (a study, a reanalysis, a decision, a statement, an investigation, an interview with a protagonist), even if it never mentions the starting point; "context" = useful background or a passing treatment; "no" = off topic, only a passing mention, a homonym, or a bare pointer to another piece.
- source_kind: what the item is. original_study = the research paper itself or its preprint; press_release = an institution announcing its own work; relay = a short re-post of someone else's piece.
- claims: at most 5, only about the nucleus, the most specific first. statement = one sentence in English, actor first, with the concrete content (what changes, who decided, what number, what is demanded). type: fact = something done or decided; position = what an actor wants or thinks; data = a measured number or ranking; finding = the result of an analysis or investigation by the source itself.
- date: the date of the event the claim describes if the item states it; null otherwise. Do not guess the year.
- quote: copy EXACTLY, character for character, a span of 10–250 characters from the item that supports the claim, in its original language. No translation, no ellipses. If none, "".
- If the item reports on a document, report, letter or investigation by others (e.g. an NGO study, a government statement, a leaked text), include it as a claim with who = its authors, and say that the item reports it.
- Never add information that is not in the item. For "no" items, claims may be empty.
