Nucleus: {NUCLEUS}

You receive: the notes (each with item_id, source, source_role, language, date, original title, relevance, adds, claims with ids). Return only JSON with exactly these keys:

Form:
- title: names the open question or the conflict of the story, at most 14 words. A question is welcome when it is the real question ("Il Consiglio ha salvato il GDPR o lo ha aperto all'intelligenza artificiale?"); otherwise a statement that contains a tension ("L'IA è già legge, sui dati i governi si dividono e decide il Parlamento"). Never a neutral description of the state of the procedure.
- summary_short: 3-4 sentences, at most 80 words: what is happening, what was decided, who objects, what comes next.
- summary_long: 2-3 paragraphs, at most 200 words in total. Background (what the package is, how it is built) goes here, not in the cards.
- key_points: at most 3. What changes or what is at stake for the reader, each with a short heading and at most 70 words.
- cards: at most 5, ordered by news value: first what was decided, revealed or documented recently, then positions, then the rest. Each card reports something that happened, was said, measured or found by a source; no card that only describes the story. title = our own clear Italian title, at most 16 words, consistent with the text (the title must not say more or less than the text). text = 40-80 words (each side of a comparison: 25-50 words). Use at most one comparison card, when two or three camps disagree on the same point; each side keeps its own sources. quote = at most one claim whose quote is worth showing; quote_translation = its Italian translation (null if already Italian).
- timeline: at most 6 dated steps that changed the story (decisions, texts, votes, publications that mattered); no announcements of intentions or events. One line each, only dates found in the claims.

Writing rules:
- Write like a good journalist: start with the fact, plain complete sentences, one idea per sentence. Neither didactic nor telegraphic.
- No editorial formulas or rhetorical devices: no "il nodo è", "la partita", "chi vince e chi perde", "da un lato... dall'altro", "in gioco c'è", "secondo le parti in campo", no announcing what the text will say, no conclusions about what it all means. Let the facts and the attributed positions carry the story.
- No isolated acronyms: spell them out the first time or avoid them. No numbers of legal articles unless explained.
- Facts, numbers, dates, positions and quotes come ONLY from the claims you cite in support, with the same strength: if a claim says someone voted against, write that they voted against, not that they "did not vote in favour". You may add context (what something is) but never new facts: no numbers, dates or people that are not in the notes.
- Select. Few understandable, well-founded pieces are better than a collage. Plurality yes, exhibited no.
- Give precedence to: primary documents and comparisons between texts; information that only one source brings; verifiable data; investigations; positions of the actors who decide. Ignore items with relevance "no".
- Every key point, card, comparison side and timeline step must cite at least one claim in support, written as "item_id:claim_id".

Style examples. They come from ANOTHER story and only show tone and density. Never reuse their content.
