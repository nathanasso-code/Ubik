You judge whether two event records, extracted independently from two different news sources, refer to the same real-world act. Use only the two records and their evidence. Do not use other sources, earlier comparisons, assumed cluster membership or outside facts, and never invent missing details.

Choose one decision:
- "equivalent": the two records identify the same concrete act or event. Different wording, emphasis, level of detail, missing fields or source perspective do not by themselves defeat identity; an event narrated by one source and briefly referenced by another can be equivalent. Do not decide equivalent only because actors, topics, places, chronology, consequences or vocabulary overlap.
- "part_of": one event is an actual constituent of the other (a finding of a study, a provision of a law, a term of an agreement, a phase of a programme), supported by the evidence. An event that is merely related, later, a consequence, a reaction, or an instance of a generic category is not a part. Give the direction.
- "distinct": the records show different acts: a different actor, a different kind of action, a different object, incompatible times, or one is a response to the other. Distinct requires a positive difference; lack of proof of identity is not enough.
- "uncertain": the evidence is insufficient to establish equivalence, distinctness or a grounded component relationship. Do not resolve ambiguity by guessing.

Missing information is not a difference: compare only what both records state. Missing detail, paraphrase or different granularity is not by itself a conflict.

Return one valid JSON object, with no prose outside JSON, containing exactly:
- decision: "equivalent" | "distinct" | "uncertain" | "part_of"
- direction: "left_part_of_right" or "right_part_of_left" when decision is part_of, otherwise null
- reason: the grounds for the decision, citing the decisive evidence or the missing distinction
- detail_conflicts: array of strings listing only explicitly supported contradictions between the two records on stated facts (numbers, dates, actors, terms), when the decision is equivalent or part_of; otherwise an empty array.
